What changed in verification

Kernel checking establishes that a proof has its formal type. The surrounding checker must also establish that it checked the intended sources, statements and outputs. The phase 13 repair strengthened those connections: it reads Rocq source syntax, records compiler runs and output hashes, compares statements with their accepted versions, and checks resolved names. The annotated release records for phase13, phase14 and shifted report checks in two fresh clones and agreement on all 54 repaired-side regression tests.[10][11][12]

The limits remain part of the result. Universe constraints are not compared. The identity layer checks which names occur; frozen sources and characterizations protect the definitions behind those names. Reproducibility was exercised with one Docker image. Ten attack demonstrations establish acceptance of attack text by the old checker, not kernel acceptance of a false theorem; two other attacks have coverage records rather than reproductions. 02 / Establishing the outcome

The original three clauses were proved.
The faithfulness question is separate.

The target is one formal theorem with three main clauses. For independent standard Gaussian points in dimension d, the sample density is n(d)/d2. The fitting equation is xiTSxi = d. The statements allow arbitrary sample-size sequences subject to the specified eventual density bounds.[2]

03 / The method

Fix the mathematical interface.
Let the proof decomposition evolve.

The most consequential decision was the change in review scope. The user wanted to review the main theorems and the definitions that give them meaning, rather than a long sequence of intermediate challenges.

On 30 September, the work was reorganized into one main-statement package with three clauses and a shared glossary. A separate session authored the statement; the final results and policy modules were written by the AWS master solver. Internal lemmas could be split, replaced and reassigned while the recorded main statement and its five trusted dependencies stayed unchanged.[2][6]

  1. 1Fix the targetMain clauses, Gaussian law, event definitions and quantifier order.
  2. 2Assign bounded workOne owner per module family, explicit dependencies and expected conclusions.
  3. 3Develop and saveInteractive goals, library searches, tactic trials and saved source proofs.
  4. 4Check integrationsCompile, inspect every result’s assumptions, then run the kernel checker.
  5. 5Check the exact targetRebuild the final closure and check theorem and policy against the fixed type.

The analytic side

Gaussian identities, smoothing, concentration and covariance comparison supplied probability estimates. Finite nets and orientation arguments then controlled whole families of matrix candidates. Library bridges made the concrete Gaussian product measure agree with the matrix and scalar representations used in the proofs.

The spectral side

Non-backtracking walks, meaning walks that do not immediately reverse an edge, supported moment and trace bounds. These were combined with recurrence-error control and Chebyshev polynomial estimates to obtain a positive lower spectral bound. That bound fed the construction of well-conditioned fits.

The final step moved from fixed positive densities to arbitrary sample-size sequences by checked padding and monotonicity reductions. This preserved the order “choose the spectral constants, then quantify over all sequences.” The fitting and balanced-alternative clauses were assembled from these limits and the already checked supercritical results.[3][8]

A crucial distinction: an intermediate theorem can pass an axiom audit while still taking an unproved mathematical premise as an argument. The journals kept those premises visible. Completion was declared only after the actual probability laws supplied them and the final theorem matched the fixed statement.

Evidence of useful cross-review

Lancelot found a representation problem before final assembly.

One module expressed the scalar threshold as a cast of a natural-number product; another expressed it as a product of real numbers. Treating them as equal by computation at the concrete index could unfold a natural-number multiplication involving 3,145,740. Lancelot recommended a symbolic equality for arbitrary indices instead.

The final paper_main_squarefree_lower_tau lemma uses that algebraic bridge. The review records the issue as resolved in the integrated lower-probability module. This is a specific, traceable benefit of another agent reading the composition. It is not a measured saving in minutes.[5]

Assessment: all three workers were useful in the ordinary, observable sense: their work survived integration and was consumed by the proof. Parallel work was feasible because module ownership and interfaces were explicit. How much elapsed time it saved remains unmeasured.

05 / Interactive proof support

Rocq MCP was a working instrument,
not just an installed dependency.

The server, rocq-mcp-evolve, exposed Rocq’s live proof state to the agents. They could open a theorem, inspect its goals, submit tactics, try alternatives, roll back and check a candidate proof. This gave direct feedback on each proposed proof step. The logs establish use, but they do not measure the incremental time saved over a compiler-only workflow.

Where the server helped

  • Rocq exposed the actual remaining goal after tactic attempts.
  • Agents tried several alternatives and recovered previous states without rebuilding the entire library for every step.
  • Whole-file diagnostics located elaboration and import failures.
  • Library queries and short proof experiments informed saved source changes.

What it did not establish

  • Interactive completion did not prove that the saved source was complete.
  • A later theorem could open despite earlier failures admitted within the session.
  • The historical notebook records a “no holes” build message for a file that still contained an admitted theorem.
  • Import success on a trivial smoke file did not guarantee the correct project load path.

The effective combination was MCP plus independent checking. MCP supported exploration. CLI compilation checked saved files. Assumption audits and rocq check checked the resulting proof libraries. The final comparison checked both the theorem and the policy target against the fixed statement.[1][6]

A useful discrepancy between prescribed and observed practice: the instructions encouraged auto_close first on every new goal, but the counted native sessions contain only four such calls, against 929 opens. The observed workflow was mainly driven by step, try, check and build. This count does not include automation written inside tactics or invoked through shell transports.

08 / Interpretation

A successful case study,
not a controlled experiment.

Supported by the records

  • All three workers produced retained, integrated proof contributions.
  • At least one cross-agent review identified a specific issue resolved in the final source.
  • MCP was used extensively by Arthur and Marcol, and concretely by Lancelot and the master.
  • A shared environment and layered checks supported reproducible integration.
  • The final merged sources match the recorded verified proof.

Not established here

  • A speedup factor for three workers versus one.
  • The marginal time or monetary saving due to MCP.
  • A ranking of models or agents from unequal assignments.
  • Total project cost, total human effort or a reliable all-session token bill.
  • Human-verified faithfulness to the paper, or protocol acceptance.

Tool counts measure interaction, not mathematical progress. A difficult theorem can require many unsuccessful calls; a short assembly proof can depend on hundreds of earlier results. Module and commit counts are similarly unsuitable as stand-alone productivity measures.

Recommended method for a future formalization

Keep one stable main-statement package and a small human review surface. Use a proof-writing master with two substantial workers, adding a third when a concrete independent task is ready. Give each worker its own source tree and proof state. Prepare dependencies, require task acknowledgements, and budget memory before adding concurrency. Keep compiler and kernel checks separate from interactive diagnostics.

To evaluate efficiency rather than only completion, record task start/finish times, dependency waiting, failed attempts, model usage and integration time from the beginning. Compare matched bounded tasks with and without MCP, or with one versus several workers. Treat the present evidence as motivation for those experiments, not as their result.[9]
