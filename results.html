Original Gaussian results

Three Gaussian ellipsoid-fitting results

The probability of a positive semidefinite fit tends to one below density 1/4 and to zero above it. Below the threshold, fits can also have trace d and fixed positive spectral bounds. Balanced positive-definite combinations of sample outer products have the opposite transition.

Hypotheses and scope. Independent standard Gaussian points in dimension d; sample sizes n(d). The fitting transition uses limsup n/d² < 1/4 or liminf n/d² > 1/4. For every alpha < 1/4, the well-conditioned result assumes limsup n/d² ≤ alpha; its spectral constants depend only on alpha.

One formal theorem bundles three mathematical results and includes measurability of their events. No claim at the critical density follows from these threshold statements. Formalization of the three main results of Misiakiewicz and Wen.

Extensions

Universality and finite or countable mixtures

A sharp fitting transition at alpha_*(kappa), bounded-condition positive definite fits below it, and a deterministic limiting fitting error extend from independent coordinates to finite and countable mixtures of product laws.

Hypotheses and scope. Samples are independent and identically distributed; n/d² tends to alpha > 0. Within each mixture component, coordinates are independent, mean zero, variance one, and uniformly subgaussian with one constant K > 0. Every coordinate has unconditional fourth moment kappa > 1 after averaging over the mixture. Mixture families and their weights may depend on dimension.

Coordinates of a point may be dependent under the mixture. A component need not itself have fourth moment kappa. The independent-coordinate result formalizes Koehler and Sohn; the mixture extension is stated separately.

Extensions

The optimal condition number

For every C > 1, the probability of an exact fit with condition number at most C has threshold alpha(C,kappa). Below alpha_*(kappa), the optimal condition number converges in probability to C_*(alpha,kappa), the inverse curve.

Hypotheses and scope. Admissible finite or countable mixtures of product laws, with common subgaussian bound K > 0 and unconditional fourth moment kappa > 1. n/d² tends to alpha > 0; convergence of the optimum assumes 0 < alpha < alpha_*(kappa).

Nothing is asserted at alpha = alpha(C,kappa). The condition number is the matrix eigenvalue ratio; the ellipsoid semiaxis ratio is its square root. At kappa = 3, alpha_*(3) = 1/4.

Extensions

Robustness to small changes of law

The condition-number threshold and limiting optimum persist when the point law is within o(d⁻²) in total variation of an admissible finite or countable mixture.

Hypotheses and scope. Reference families satisfy the mixture assumptions with kappa > 1 and K > 0. d² times the total-variation discrepancy tends to zero. The threshold comparison is away from alpha(C,kappa); the optimum limit is below alpha_*(kappa).

A separate contamination example shows that O(d⁻²) cannot replace o(d⁻²) in general.

Extensions

Explicit dependent point laws

Paired coordinates and a single latent sign shared across all coordinates yield explicit dependent laws with sharp fitting transitions. The latent-sign theorem also gives the limiting fitting error and small-residual interpolation.

Hypotheses and scope. Paired-law parameter −1 ≤ eps ≤ 7/3. Latent-sign parameter 0 ≤ s ≤ 1, allowed to depend on dimension; the unconditional coordinate fourth moment is 9/5. Independent samples with n/d² tending to a positive density.

The condition and interpolation constants in the latent-sign result are uniform over s in [0,1].

Extensions

Dependence and the limits of the mixture model

For the latent-sign law, variance-tensorization constants can grow exponentially with dimension, despite exact fitting below the threshold. A continuous latent mixture can remain at total-variation distance one from every countable mixture of product laws.

Hypotheses and scope. The exponential lower-bound result concerns a fixed latent-sign parameter s > 0 in the specified latent-sign family. The matching upper-bound theorem covers 0 ≤ s ≤ 1. The continuous-mixture result concerns its explicitly constructed law; it is not a claim about every continuous mixture.

The upper tensorization bound is 2 ((1+s)/(1−3s/7))^d.

Translated samples

S1 · Small Gaussian shifts preserve the optimum

The centered-Gaussian condition-number threshold and limiting optimum are unchanged by deterministic shifts small compared with sqrt(d).

Hypotheses and scope. Independent N(m_d,I_d) points; n/d² → alpha > 0. ||m_d||/sqrt(d) → 0; C > 1 for the threshold. The optimal-condition limit assumes 0 < alpha < 1/4.

Equality at the condition-number threshold is left open.

Translated samples

S2 · Arbitrary Gaussian shifts: the cost of translation

Below density 1/4, positive definite fits exist and the optimal condition number is of order 1 + ||m_d||/sqrt(d). A lower bound on that scale holds uniformly over translations at every positive density.

Hypotheses and scope. Independent standard Gaussian samples translated by a deterministic sequence m_d. n/d² → alpha > 0; the existence and two-sided bounds require alpha < 1/4. No bound is imposed on the shift magnitudes.

The lower bound holds on one high-probability event for all translations. The upper/existence statement applies to each deterministic shift sequence.

S3 · Above the Gaussian threshold: no PSD quadric

One high-probability event excludes every quadric whose quadratic part is positive semidefinite and nonzero, whatever its linear and constant terms. It therefore excludes a centered fit after every translation.

Hypotheses and scope. Independent standard Gaussian points; n/d² → alpha > 1/4.

The event is chosen before the quadric; this is simultaneous over all such quadrics.

S4 · Fitting error is unchanged by Gaussian shifts

At every positive density, arbitrary deterministic shifts preserve the limiting fitting error e_*(alpha,3). Taking the infimum over all translations has the same limit.

Hypotheses and scope. Independent standard Gaussian samples; n/d² → alpha > 0. Arbitrary deterministic shift sequences.

The density alpha = 1/4 is included for the error limit. A zero limiting error does not prove exact fitting at that density.

Translated samples

S5 · Symmetric mixtures exclude all PSD quadrics

Above alpha_*(kappa), symmetric mixture point laws have a positive fitting-error margin uniform over translations, and no quadric with nonzero positive semidefinite quadratic part fits all points.

Hypotheses and scope. Finite or countable admissible mixtures; kappa > 1 and a uniform subgaussian bound K > 0. The point law is centrally symmetric; individual mixture components need not be. n/d² → alpha > alpha_*(kappa).

The positive margin is chosen from kappa and alpha before K and the family. The theorem supplies measurable good events; it does not assert measurability of the existential arbitrary-quadric event.

Translated samples

S6 · Sparse shifts preserve mixture fitting

Below alpha_*(kappa), deterministic shifts supported on o(d) coordinates preserve positive definite fits with probability tending to one.

Hypotheses and scope. Finite or countable admissible mixtures; kappa > 1, K > 0, and n/d² → alpha in (0,alpha_*(kappa)). The number of nonzero shift coordinates divided by d tends to zero.

No symmetry and no bound on shift magnitudes are required. The support may change with dimension. The assertion is for each shift sequence, not one event simultaneously covering all shifts.

S7 · Opposite shifts have the same fitting statistics

For a centrally symmetric point law, a mixture of its translates by m and −m has exactly the same joint outer-product law as the translate by m. Centered-fit probabilities, optimal condition numbers and fitting errors therefore agree.

Hypotheses and scope. Independent samples from a centrally symmetric point law. Any mixture weight in [0,1], including endpoints. The stated Gaussian and admissible-mixture asymptotic consequences use the hypotheses of S1, S2, S5 or S6 as applicable.

This does not transfer arbitrary-center quadric events or an infimum over additional translations.

04 / What verification establishes

A proved statement has a precise scope.

The formal guarantee

Rocq checks that the proof establishes the stated conclusion from its hypotheses and foundational axioms. The project also checks frozen source, theorem identities, definition snapshots and reported assumptions.

The release includes fresh builds, regression tests and independent reference regeneration. The repository README describes that machinery. Formal checking and mathematical review have different roles: a correct proof does not by itself show that a statement captures every intended informal claim.

What to check before reuse

  • Read section variables and hypotheses as well as the displayed conclusion. A theorem name or a short README row is not the whole statement.
  • Distinguish the matrix condition number from the ellipsoid axis ratio, which is its square root.
  • Distinguish MathComp’s entrywise matrix norm from the separately defined Frobenius and operator norms.
  • Use Print Assumptions for the declaration you reuse. Axiom counts differ between the general library and the concrete ellipsoid theorems.

The library is not presented as having an exhaustive human review of every statement. The shifted post-release review covered named proof checks with explicit limits on rereading earlier dependencies.

Known limits of the project checker

The identity comparison records names rather than meanings, and does not compare universe constraints. Frozen source, pins and characterization checks provide separate protection for definitions. Reproducibility was demonstrated using one Docker image; that is not a claim of independent results across multiple compiler environments.

05 / Use the source

Start with one lemma.

The release uses Rocq 9.1.1, MathComp 2.6.0 and MathComp-Analysis 1.16.0. Follow the repository’s environment setup, activate the intended opam environment, and keep ROCQ_SWITCH= to use the rocq on your PATH. Choose build parallelism to fit your machine. The full make check is run in the project’s pinned Docker image, which is not published. Elsewhere it is expected to fail: its identity check requires an exporter plugin identical to the one that wrote the references in check/identity/.

git clone https://github.com/LLM4Rocq/ellipsoid_fitting_rocq.git
cd ellipsoid_fitting_rocq
git checkout shifted
# After installing and activating the documented environment:
make build ROCQ_SWITCH= JOBS=3

For example, after building with the project’s load paths:

From GaussianAnalysis.probability Require Import hanson_wright.
Check hanson_wright.
Print Assumptions hanson_wright.
