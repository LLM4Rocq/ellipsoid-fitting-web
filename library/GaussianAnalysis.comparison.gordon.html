Let x have law gauss n and y have law gauss m. For A : 'M[R]_(n, p.+1 * q.+1), X = vec_mx (x *m A) is a centered Gaussian matrix of size (p + 1) x (q + 1), whose entry X u t is the coordinate mxvec_index u t of x *m A; let Y = vec_mx (y *m B). Assume that each increment X u t - X u s within a row has a variance at most that of Y u t - Y u s, and that each increment X u t - X v s across two distinct rows has a variance at least that of Y u t - Y v s. Then E[minmax X] <= E[minmax Y], where minmax Z is the smallest over the rows of the largest entry of the row (gordon). With one row, this is the Sudakov-Fernique inequality. gordon_cov states the case of equal variances with covariances.

The proof applies Kahane's comparison inequality (gauss_comparison) to the soft min-max smm b. Its Hessian has zero row and column sums (smm_D2_rowsum, smm_D2_colsum), so its pairing with a matrix M only depends on the increments M i i + M j j - 2 * M i j (pairing_zero_sums). The Hessian is nonpositive inside a row and nonnegative across rows, which gives the sign (smm_hessian_sign). Since minmax Z - ln (p + 1) / b <= smm b z <= minmax Z + ln (q + 1) / b for Z = vec_mx z (le_smm, smm_le), letting b grow concludes.

Main results

ResultsStatementUsed by theories/
gordon
Gordon's min-max inequality: for x of law gauss n, y of law gauss m and the Gaussian matrices X = vec_mx (x A), Y = vec_mx (y B), if each increment X_ut - X_us within a row has variance at most that of Y_ut - Y_us, and each increment X_ut - X_vs across distinct rows has variance at least that of Y_ut - Y_vs, then E[min_u max_t X_ut] <= E[min_u max_t Y_ut].
gordon_cov
Gordon's inequality with equal variances: if the covariances of X are at least those of Y within each row and at most those of Y across rows, then E[min_u max_t X_ut] <= E[min_u max_t Y_ut].
pairing_zero_sumssmm_hessian_signle_smmsmm_le
If H has zero row and column sums, \sum_(i, j) M_ij H_ij = - 1/2 \sum_(i, j) H_ij (M_ii + M_jj - 2 M_ij). The soft min-max smm b z = - ln (\sum_u (\sum_t expR (b z_ut))^-1) / b is within ln (p + 1) / b below and ln (q + 1) / b above min_u max_t z_ut, and its Hessian is nonpositive within rows and nonnegative across rows.

lemma pairing_zero_sums main result source, line 532 
forall {R : realType} [n : nat] (M : 'I_n -> 'I_n -> R) [H : 'I_n -> 'I_n -> R],
(forall i : 'I_n, \sum_j H i j = 0) ->
(forall j : 'I_n, \sum_i H i j = 0) ->
\sum_i \sum_j M i j * H i j = - 2^-1 * \sum_i \sum_j H i j * (M i i + M j j - 2 * M i j)

If H has zero row and column sums, its pairing with M only depends on the increments M i i + M j j - 2 * M i j.

Main result, as the README states it

If H has zero row and column sums, \sum_(i, j) M_ij H_ij = - 1/2 \sum_(i, j) H_ij (M_ii + M_jj - 2 M_ij). The soft min-max smm b z = - ln (\sum_u (\sum_t expR (b z_ut))^-1) / b is within ln (p + 1) / b below and ln (q + 1) / b above min_u max_t z_ut, and its Hessian is nonpositive within rows and nonnegative across rows.

Gordon's min-max inequality: for x of law gauss n, y of law gauss m and the Gaussian matrices X = vec_mx (x A), Y = vec_mx (y B), if each increment X_ut - X_us within a row has variance at most that of Y_ut - Y_us, and each increment X_ut - X_vs across distinct rows has variance at least that of Y_ut - Y_vs, then E[min_u max_t X_ut] <= E[min_u max_t Y_ut].
