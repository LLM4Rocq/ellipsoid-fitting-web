For X of law normal_prob 0 s and 2 t s^2 < 1, E[expR (t X^2)] = 1 / sqrt (1 - 2 t s^2) (integral_normal_expR_sqr). For the standard normal law, E[expR (t (X^2 - 1))] = expR (- t) / sqrt (1 - 2 t), which is at most expR (t^2 / (1 - 2 |t|)) when |2 t| < 1 (integral_normal_expR_sqrB1_le), by the inequality - ln (1 - u) - u <= u^2 / (2 (1 - |u|)) for |u| < 1 (ln_subgamma).

For x of law gauss n and a row vector of weights a, centered_chi2 a x is a centered weighted chi-square variable. Its moment generating function is the product of the one-dimensional ones (integral_gauss_expR_centered_chi2) and is sub-gamma: if |a_i| <= L for every i, then E[expR (t centered_chi2 a x)] <= expR (t^2 |a|^2 / (1 - 2 |t| L)) when 2 |t| L < 1 (integral_gauss_expR_centered_chi2_le). It follows that P(|centered_chi2 a x| >= u) <= 2 expR (- u^2 / (4 (|a|^2 + L u))) for u > 0 (gauss_centered_chi2_abs_tail), with one-sided versions.
