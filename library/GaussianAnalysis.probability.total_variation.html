The total variation distance

The total variation distance of two probabilities on a measurable type is the supremum of |P A - Q A| over the measurable sets A. It is a real number in [0, 1], since probabilities are finite.

tv_dist P Qsup {|P A - Q A| : A measurable}, a real number

Main results:

  • tv_dist_ge, tv_dist_geE, tv_dist_le: |P A - Q A| <= tv_dist P Q for every measurable A (as reals and as extended reals), and tv_dist P Q is the least such bound; tv_distE: as an extended real it is the ereal_sup of the |P A - Q A|;
  • tv_dist_ge0, tv_dist_le1, tv_distC, tv_dist_triangle, tv_dist_eq0, tv_distxx: a pseudometric with values in [0, 1], zero for laws that agree on the measurable sets;
  • tuple_prod_tv: for laws mu i and nu i on T and every measurable set A of n-tuples, |tuple_prod mu n A - tuple_prod nu n A| is at most the sum of the tv_dist (mu i) (nu i), i < n (the hybrid argument of lindeberg_replacement with the indicator of A);
  • one_sub_le_expR, expR_le_one_sub, pow_one_sub_bounds: for 0 <= x < n, exp (- x n / (n - x)) <= (1 - x / n)^n <= exp (- x); pow_one_sub_dist: |(1 - x / n)^n - exp (- x)| <= x^2 / (n - x); pow_one_sub_cvg: (1 - x / n)^n tends to exp (- x) for every x >= 0.
