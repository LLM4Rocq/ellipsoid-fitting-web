Vectors x of R^M are column vectors 'cV[R]_M and h : 'rV[R]_M, as in cgmt.v; |x| is frobenius x and <h, x> is mxdot h^T x. Let K be a set of 'cV[R]_M that contains 0 and

sigma_K(h) = sup_(x in K, |x| <= 1) <h, x>              (cone_sup K h).
  • cone_sup_ub, cone_sup_le, cone_sup_ge0, cone_sup_le_norm: sigma_K(h) is the least upper bound and lies in [0, |h|]; cone_sup_lipschitz: it is 1-Lipschitz in h; cone_sup_attained: for K closed the supremum is attained (compactness); cone_sup_dot: for a cone K (a x is in K for x in K and a >= 0), <h, x> <= |x| sigma_K(h) on K.
  • cone_sup_proj: for a closed convex cone K of row vectors, sigma_(tr_set K)(h) = |proj K h| (cone_proj_support of moreau.v), and cone_sup_tr_setE writes sigma_(tr_set K)(h) as a supremum over K.

Let B be a set of 'cV[R]_M such that every nonzero point of K has a positive multiple in B (a base of K when B is in K and 0 is not in B). The auxiliary value of the CGMT on B (cgmt.v),

phi(B) = cgmt_aux B g h = inf_(x in B) (|g| |x| - <h, x>)_+,

is governed by the sign of |g| - sigma_K(h):

  • cgmt_aux_cone_zero: for K closed, phi(B) = 0 when 0 < sigma_K(h) and |g| <= sigma_K(h), in particular when 0 < |g| <= sigma_K(h) or |g| < sigma_K(h) (cgmt_aux_cone_zero_lt): at a point x of K with |x| <= 1 and <h, x> = sigma_K(h), the multiples a x with a > 0 have |g| |a x| - <h, a x> <= 0;
  • cgmt_aux_cone_ge: for a cone K, B nonempty and in K, and r <= |x| on B, phi(B) >= r (|g| - sigma_K(h))_+, since <h, x> <= |x| sigma_K(h) on K.

cgmt_aux_tr_cone_zero and cgmt_aux_tr_cone_ge are the same statements for a closed convex cone K and a set B of row vectors, with |proj K h| in place of sigma_K(h); tr_set0, tr_set_cone, tr_set_base, tr_set_sub and tr_set_norm_ge carry the hypotheses from row vectors to tr_set.

With the CGMT of cgmt.v: let a have law gauss (N * M), so that A = vec_mx a is an N x M matrix of independent standard normal entries, z have law gauss (N + M), g = lsubmx z and h = rsubmx z, N > 0, B be nonempty and |x| <= rho on B.

  • cone_escape_zero_tail: if rho > 0, K is closed, B is closed and convex and P(|g| < sigma_K(h)) > 1/2, then inf_(x in B) |A x| is at least rho (1 + 2 sqrt (ln 4)) + u with probability at most 2 exp (- u^2 / (2 rho^2)) (cgmt_zero_tail): the kernel of A meets K approximately.
  • cone_escape_lower_tail: if K is a cone, B is in K and r <= |x| on B, then inf_(x in B) |A x| is at most r E (|g| - sigma_K(h))_+ - rho - u with probability at most 2 exp (- u^2 / (2 rho^2)) (cgmt_lower and cgmt_escape_tail): the kernel of A stays away from B when |g| exceeds sigma_K(h) in mean by more than (rho + u) / r.

Main result, as the README states it

The CGMT on cones, for A = vec_mx a an N x M matrix of independent standard normal entries (N > 0), z = (g, h) of law gauss (N + M) and B as above, nonempty with |x| <= rho on B. If rho > 0, K is closed, B is closed and convex and P(|g| < sigma_K(h)) > 1/2, then inf_(x in B) |A x| >= rho (1 + 2 sqrt (ln 4)) + u has probability at most 2 exp (- u^2 / (2 rho^2)): the kernel of A meets K approximately. If B is in K and r <= |x| on B, then inf_(x in B) |A x| <= r E (|g| - sigma_K(h))_+ - rho - u has probability at most 2 exp (- u^2 / (2 rho^2)): the kernel stays away from B.
