For x of law gauss n and A an n x k matrix, x *m A is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance A^T *m A. Its law depends only on that covariance: if A^T *m A = B^T *m B, with B an m x k matrix, then gauss n [set x | X (x *m A)] = gauss m [set y | X (y *m B)] for every measurable set X of 'rV[R]_k (gauss_mulmx_law). When the columns of A are orthonormal, x *m A has law gauss k (gauss_orthogonal_law); in particular gauss n is invariant under every orthogonal matrix. The integral forms are ge0_integral_gauss_mulmx and integral_gauss_orthogonal.

The proof goes through test functions. Kahane's comparison inequality, applied in both directions, gives equal expectations to every test of class C^2 with polynomially bounded derivatives (gauss_law_smooth). A bounded Lipschitz function G is the uniform limit of its double Gaussian smoothings gauss_smooth t (gauss_smooth t G), which are of class C^2 because Gaussian integration by parts moves the derivative inside the second smoothing (gauss_smooth_partial_stein); hence the expectations of G agree too (gauss_law_lipschitz). Finally a finite measure on 'rV[R]_k is determined by the integrals of the Lipschitz functions with values in [0, 1] (measure_lipschitz_unique): the sets {Phi > 0}, for Phi nonnegative and Lipschitz, are closed under intersection, generate the Borel sets (measurable_lipschitz_pos), and their indicators are nondecreasing limits of min 1 (i Phi).

lipschitz_posthe sets [set x | 0 < Phi x], for Phi : 'rV[R]_k -> R nonnegative and Lipschitz for the Euclidean norm

Main result, as the README states it

The law of a centered Gaussian vector depends only on its covariance: for x of law gauss n and y of law gauss m, if A^T *m A = B^T *m B, then P(x *m A \in X) = P(y *m B \in X) for every measurable set X of 'rV[R]_k, and E[f (x *m A)] = E[f (y *m B)] for every measurable f.
