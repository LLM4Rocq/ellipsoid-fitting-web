Rademacher signs are written as a finite average over the 2^n sign vectors, not as a measure. For independent random elements X_1, ..., X_n (tuple_prod) and a finite class of functions f_j, the tools of empirical process theory that the small-ball method needs, for expectations only: no concentration inequality is used.

Main results:

  • rademacher_mean_cst, rademacher_meanD, rademacher_meanZ, ler_rademacher_mean, rademacher_mean_flip, rademacher_mean_neg: the average over signs is linear, monotone, and invariant under changing one sign or all of them; rademacher_mean_prod: E eps_k eps_l = [k == l]; rademacher_mean_sqr_sum, rademacher_mean_abs_sum: E (sum_k a_k eps_k)^2 = sum_k a_k^2, hence E |sum_k a_k eps_k| <= (sum_k a_k^2)^(1/2).
  • Contraction (Ledoux-Talagrand), contraction: for a finite class of vectors t_j of R^n and L-Lipschitz phi_k with phi_k(0) = 0,
    rademacher_mean (max_j |sum_k eps_k phi_k(t_jk)|)
  <= 2 L rademacher_mean (max_j |sum_k eps_k t_jk|);

    contraction_bigmax0: the same without absolute values and without the factor 2, over the class and the zero vector, proved one coordinate at a time (bigmax0_contraction1).

  • Symmetrization (Gine-Zinn), symmetrization: for x of law tuple_prod mu n and measurable f_j integrable for every mu_k,
    E max_j |sum_k (f_j(x_k) - E f_j(x_k))|
  <= 2 E rademacher_mean (max_j |sum_k eps_k f_j(x_k)|),

    as integrals in [0, +oo]. Proof: an independent copy as the second block of n + n coordinates of law doubled_law mu n (ge0_integral_tuple_left, ge0_integral_tuple_right); Jensen's inequality for max_norm (jensen_max_norm), one coordinate of the copy at a time, and the exchange of x_k and its copy (ge0_integral_swap), both by Fubini at one coordinate (ge0_integral_tuple_prod_set of efron_stein.v).

  • Tools: max_normD, max_normN (max_norm is a norm), measurable_bigmax0, measurable_max_norm, ge0_integral_rademacher_mean, measurable_rademacher_mean.
