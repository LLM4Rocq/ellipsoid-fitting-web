GaussianAnalysis/random_matrix/

random_matrix/

GaussianAnalysis.random_matrix7 files7,328 lines315 lemmas and theorems, 56 definitions

The Gaussian orthogonal ensemble: the Schwinger-Dyson equation for its moments, the operator norm, Wigner's semicircle law, and the invariance of its law under orthogonal conjugation, with the diagonal of equivariant matrix functions; the restricted isometry of matrices with independent subexponential columns (Adamczak, Litvak, Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann) and the norms of sparse combinations of subgaussian vectors; the trace moments and the operator norm of Gaussian matrix series, and the sharp upper edge of the GOE (its largest eigenvalue has mean at most 2 and Gaussian tails above it).

Guide

Written for this documentation from the sources and the README, and checked against the Rocq statements. The file headers and the statements are the reference.

This directory studies random matrices over an arbitrary realType, mostly the Gaussian orthogonal ensemble. For a of law gauss (d * d), goe_mx a is the symmetric matrix W = (A + A^T) / sqrt (2 d) with A = vec_mx a; its entries W_ij, i <= j, are independent centered normals of variance 1 / d off the diagonal and 2 / d on it. As a function of the Gaussian vector a, W falls under the tools of gaussian/ and comparison/.

The GOE files give the Schwinger-Dyson equation, Wigner's semicircle law, the invariance under orthogonal conjugation, tails of the entries and the sharp upper edge. Two files leave the GOE: Gaussian matrix series sum_j g_j H_j, with bounds free of the dimension, and the restricted isometry of independent subexponential vectors (Adamczak, Litvak, Pajor and Tomczak-Jaegermann; ALPT below).

Reading order

  1. goe.v: the GOE matrix, Gaussian integration by parts, the Schwinger-Dyson equation, operator-norm and trace-variance bounds.
  2. wigner.v: the convergence of the moments and Wigner's semicircle law.
  3. goe_rotation.v: invariance under orthogonal conjugation, applied to equivariant matrix functions.
  4. goe_entries.v: tails of the entries and of the row sums of squares.
  5. goe_edge.v: the sharp upper edge of the spectrum.
  6. matrix_series.v: Gaussian matrix series.
  7. restricted_isometry.v: restricted isometry, and norms of sparse combinations of independent vectors.

Core definitions

  • goe_mx (goe.v): (A + A^T) / sqrt (2 d) with A = vec_mx a.
  • polyfun (goe.v): F is continuous with |F x| <= C (1 + |x|^k).
  • mxpow_deriv (goe.v): sum_(r < k) W^r E W^(k-1-r), the derivative of M |-> M^k at W along E.
  • goe_stat (wigner.v): (1 / d) sum_i f (spec W i).
  • conj_equivariant, diag_var (goe_rotation.v): F (Q W Q^T) = Q F(W) Q^T for Q orthogonal and W symmetric; the empirical variance of the diagonal.
  • sym_dilation (matrix_series.v): [[0, B], [B^T, 0]] for a rectangular B.
  • gram_off, rip_const (restricted_isometry.v): sum_(i <> j) u_i v_j <x_i, x_j>; the supremum of |gram_off x u u| over the m-sparse u with |u| <= 1.

Main results

Below, a has law gauss (d * d) and W = goe_mx a.

Moments and the semicircle law.

  • goe_schwinger_dyson: E tr W^(k+1) = (k E tr W^(k-1) + sum_(r < k) E[tr W^r tr W^(k-1-r)]) / d.
  • goe_moment_cvg: E tr W^k / d tends, as d grows, to the k-th moment of semicircle_prob 2.
  • wigner: for f continuous on R and L-Lipschitz on [-3, 3] and eps > 0, P(|goe_stat f a - int f d(semicircle_prob 2)| >= eps) <= eps for every large d. wigner_uniform holds uniformly over t in [t0, t1] for continuous f_t that are L-Lipschitz in x and in t on [-3, 3], with the exceptional set inside a measurable event.

Edge and entries.

  • goe_lambda_max_mean, goe_lambda_maxN_mean: for d >= 1, E lambda_max W <= 2 and E lambda_max (- W) <= 2.
  • goe_lambda_max_tail, goe_opnorm_edge_tail: for d >= 1 and t >= 0, P(lambda_max W >= 2 + t) <= exp (- d t^2 / 4) and P(opnorm W >= 2 + t) <= 2 exp (- d t^2 / 4).
  • goe_mx_offdiag_tail, goe_mx_row_tail: for i != j and s >= 0, P(|W_ij| >= s) <= 2 exp (- d s^2 / 2); for u > 0, P(sum_(j != i) W_ij^2 >= 1 + u) <= exp (- d u^2 / (4 (1 + u))).

Rotation invariance.

  • goe_conj_integral: E F(Q^T W Q) = E F(W) for Q orthogonal and a |-> F (goe_mx a) measurable.
  • goe_diag_var_tail: for F conj_equivariant, symmetric on symmetric matrices, with a |-> F (goe_mx a) measurable, C >= 0 and eps > 0, P(diag_var F(W) >= eps) <= 2 C / ((d + 2) eps) + P(|F(W)|_F^2 > C d).

Matrix series and sparse vectors.

  • gauss_mx_series_moment: for symmetric H_j, S = sum_j H_j^2, g of law gauss r and q >= 1, E tr (sum_j g_j H_j)^(2q) <= (2q - 1)!! opnorm S^(q-1) tr S.
  • gauss_mx_series_opnorm_log: for rectangular B_j and a real d >= 1 with opnorm (sum_j B_j B_j^T) and opnorm (sum_j B_j^T B_j) at most sqrt d and tr (sum_j B_j B_j^T) <= d, E opnorm (sum_j g_j B_j) <= 5 d^(1/4) sqrt (ln (2 + d)).
  • restricted_isometry: let x_1, ..., x_N in 'rV_n have law tuple_prod mu N, every <x_i, y> with |y| = 1 centered and subexponential with constant psi > 0. For 1 <= m <= N, 0 < beta <= 1/2 and K >= 1, outside a measurable event of probability at most 2 (floor (log_4 m) + 2) exp (- K sqrt m (1 + ln (N / m))), rip_const m x <= 256 T^2 + 32 T C + 8 beta C^2, with T = 2100 K psi sqrt m (1 + ln (N / m) - ln beta) and C = max_i |x_i|.
  • sparse_norm_subgaussian: if instead the <x_i, y> are centered and subgaussian with constant K > 0, then for 1 <= m <= N and s >= 0, outside a measurable event of probability at most 2 exp (- s), every m-sparse u has |sum_i u_i x_i| <= 8 K sqrt (m (1 + ln (N / m)) + 6 (m + n) + s) |u|.

How the proofs go

  • goe_schwinger_dyson: Gaussian integration by parts in each coordinate of a (gauss_ibp_polyfun) and the derivative of matrix powers (is_derive_mxpow).
  • goe_moment_cvg: induction on k with the Schwinger-Dyson recursion goe_tr_sd, whose covariance terms tend to 0 (goe_tr_cov_cvg); semicircle_catalan, from segner, gives the limit recursion for the semicircle moments.
  • wigner: off the event opnorm W >= 3, Bernstein polynomials (weierstrass_lipschitz) handle the mean and Gaussian concentration the fluctuations.
  • goe_lambda_max_mean: the Sudakov-Fernique inequality compares x_j^T W x_j, linear in a, with (2 / sqrt d) <g, x_j>, g of law gauss d, on finite families of unit vectors, and a net reaches the sphere; Gaussian concentration with the sharp constant gives the tails.
  • goe_equiv_form: a Householder reflection replaces the Haar measure.
  • gauss_mx_series_moment: integration by parts gives the recursion gauss_mx_series_trace_rec; sym_dilation handles rectangular matrices.
  • restricted_isometry follows ALPT with value levels instead of sorted coordinates: conditional Bernstein bounds (cross_tail), decoupling (gram_off_decouple), nets and a union bound.

Connections

The directory requires gaussian/, comparison/, seven files of matrix/, fourier/charfun_rV.v, probability/semicircle_law.v, and for restricted_isometry.v the product laws, subgaussian variables and conditioning of probability/.

matrix/tensor_packing.v uses matrix_series.v. applications/ uses matrix_series.v for T1, goe.v and goe_edge.v for T3, and goe.v, goe_rotation.v, goe_edge.v and goe_entries.v for T4. extension/ uses goe.v, wigner.v, goe_rotation.v and restricted_isometry.v; theories/ does not use the directory.

matrix_series.v
gauss_mx_series_momentgauss_mx_series_trace_recgauss_mx_series_ibpgauss_mx_series_trace_sqr
Gaussian matrix series. For symmetric H_1, ..., H_r in 'M[R]_n, S = \sum_j H_j^2 and g of law gauss r, the series X = \sum_j g_j H_j satisfies E tr X^(2q) <= (2q - 1)!! opnorm S^(q-1) tr S for every q >= 1, with no dimension in the bound (an integral in the extended reals). It follows from E tr X^2 = tr S, which needs no symmetry, and the recursion E tr X^(2q+2) <= (2q + 1) opnorm S E tr X^(2q). The recursion comes from Gaussian integration by parts in each coordinate, E tr X^(k+1) = \sum_j E tr (H_j D_j) with D_j the derivative of X^k along H_j. Lemma 4.2 of the fourfold ergodic paper of family 154 of the OpenAI math collection (revision 1581d4cbd).

If the two operator norms are at most sqrt d and tr (\sum_j B_j B_j^T) <= d for a real d >= 1, then E opnorm (\sum_j g_j B_j) <= 5 d^(1/4) sqrt (ln (2 + d)). No dimension of the matrices enters.
