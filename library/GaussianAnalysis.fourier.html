GaussianAnalysis/fourier/

fourier/

GaussianAnalysis.fourier6 files5,771 lines196 lemmas and theorems, 21 definitions

Characteristic functions of laws on the line and on row vectors, written without complex numbers; Levy concentration functions, Esseen's inequality and the Kolmogorov-Rogozin inequality under a second moment condition; Gaussian regularization and the bound of probabilities by the integral of the characteristic function; the vector small-ball estimate of Koehler and Sohn; the Fourier inversion theorem.

Guide

Written for this documentation from the sources and the README, and checked against the Rocq statements. The file headers and the statements are the reference.

This directory does Fourier analysis of probability laws without complex numbers, which MathComp-Analysis lacks. A characteristic function is a pair of real integrals, E[cos (t X)] and E[sin (t X)], and a pair (a, b) stands for a + i b, with the complex product cmul and modulus cmod.

Laws on the line are probabilities on measurableTypeR R, the type of normal_prob. Independence is a product measure, P1 \x P2 or tuple_prod mu n (probability/lindeberg.v). The law of a sum or an image is a parameter nu, assumed to be that image measure. Here <x, theta> is mxdot x theta and |x| is the Euclidean norm frobenius x.

The files lead to the vector small-ball estimate of Koehler and Sohn (arXiv 2608.27372, Proposition 2.4), used in the satisfiable phase of the universality proof in extension/. On the way they prove Esseen's inequality, a concentration bound for independent sums, and a bound of probabilities by the integral of |cf|, without inversion. The last file proves Fourier inversion on row vectors.

Reading order

  1. charfun.v: characteristic functions on the line: images, affine maps, independent sums, symmetrization, the normal law.
  2. esseen.v: the Levy concentration function, Esseen's inequality, the concentration of sums.
  3. gauss_factor.v: Lemma 3.5 of Koehler and Sohn and its first half.
  4. charfun_rV.v: characteristic functions on 'rV[R]_m, the Gaussian case, independent sums, Gaussian regularization.
  5. small_ball.v: Lemma 3.8 of Koehler and Sohn and the vector small-ball estimate.
  6. inversion.v: the Fourier inversion theorem on 'rV[R]_m.

Core definitions

  • cf_re, cf_im, cf, cf_norm (charfun.v): E[cos (t X)] and E[sin (t X)] for X of law mu on the line, their pair and its modulus.
  • cf_rV_re, cf_rV_im, cf_rV, cf_rV_norm (charfun_rV.v): the same for a law on 'rV[R]_m, with cos <x, theta> and sin <x, theta>.
  • conc_fun (esseen.v): the Levy concentration function Q(mu, L) = sup_a mu [a, a + L].
  • sym_law (gauss_factor.v): the law of X - X' for independent X, X' of law mu.
  • cf_rV_density (inversion.v): (2 pi)^-m \int (cos <theta, w> cf_rV_re nu theta + sin <theta, w> cf_rV_im nu theta) dtheta. cf_rV_inv_prob is the probability with this density, when |cf_rV nu| is integrable.

Main results

On the line.

  • cf_sym, cf_normal: the law of X - X', for X, X' independent of law mu, has the characteristic function (cf_norm mu t ^ 2, 0); for nonzero s, cf (normal_prob m s) t = exp (- (s t)^2 / 2) (cos (m t), sin (m t)).

Concentration.

  • esseen: Q(mu, 2 pi / T) <= pi^2 / (4 T) \int_{-T}^{T} cf_norm mu t dt for every law mu and T > 0.
  • conc_fun_sum: for n >= 1, v, lambda, eps > 0, L >= 0 and independent x_1, ..., x_n of laws mu_k with E[x_k^2] <= v and Q(mu_k, lambda) <= 1 - eps, the law nu of the sum satisfies Q(nu, L) <= 2 pi^2 sqrt pi (L + sqrt (v / eps)) / (lambda sqrt (eps n)).

Lemma 3.5 of Koehler and Sohn.

  • gauss_factor_le: for v, lambda, eps > 0, a law mu with E[X^2] <= v and Q(mu, lambda) <= 1 - eps, 0 < r <= 1/2 and r <= s, \int |cf mu u|^(1/r^2) exp (- s^2 u^2 / (2 r^2)) du <= r gauss_factor_const v lambda eps.
  • conc_fun_truncated_level: a law with mean 0, E[X^2] = 1 and E[X^2 1_{|X| <= M}] >= c, M > 0, has Q(mu, L) <= 1 - min ((c - 4 L^2) / M^2, L^2 / (2 L^2 + 1)) for every L > 0.

On row vectors.

  • cf_rV_gauss, cf_rV_tuple_prod: cf_rV (gauss m) theta = (exp (- |theta|^2 / 2), 0); if nu is the law of sum_k x_k w_k under tuple_prod mu n, cf_rV nu theta is the complex product of the cf (mu k) <w_k, theta>.
  • prod_gauss_le_cf_rV: for Y of law nu, an independent G of law gauss m, s > 0 and B measurable, P(Y + s G in B) <= (2 pi)^-m (\int exp (- s^2 |theta|^2 / 2) |cf_rV nu theta| dtheta) lebesgue_rV m B.
  • ball_le_cf_rV: if \int |cf_rV nu| < +oo, the same bound holds with s = 0 for P(Y in B), B a closed Euclidean ball of radius r > 0.

Small balls.

  • vector_small_ball: for v, lambda, eps > 0 there is C > 0 such that, if x has law tuple_prod mu n with E[x_k^2] <= v and Q(mu_k, lambda) <= 1 - eps, and the w_k in 'rV[R]_m satisfy sum_k <y, w_k>^2 >= |y|^2 for all y and |w_k| <= rho, rho > 0, then P(|sum_k x_k w_k - z| <= eta sqrt m) <= (C eta)^m for all eta >= C rho and all z.
  • vector_small_ball_moments: the same for laws with mean 0, E[x^2] = 1 and E[x^2 1_{|x| <= M}] >= 1/2, with C depending only on M > 0, as in Proposition 2.4.

Inversion.

  • cf_rV_inversion: if \int |cf_rV nu| < +oo against lebesgue_rV m, then nu X = \int_X cf_rV_density nu for every measurable X; the density is continuous, nonnegative and at most (2 pi)^-m \int |cf_rV nu| (continuous_cf_rV_density, cf_rV_density_ge0, cf_rV_density_le).

How the proofs go

  • esseen uses fejer T, half the Fejer kernel, which fejer_ge bounds below by 2 T / pi^2 for |z| <= pi / T (Jordan's inequality). conc_fun_sum writes |cf nu| as a product (cf_norm_tuple_prod), bounds each factor through the symmetrization X - X' (sym_cf_bound) and applies esseen0.
  • gauss_factor_le bounds |cf mu|^(1/r^2) by the characteristic function of a sum of floor (1 / (2 r^2)) independent copies of sym_law mu (cf_re_sum_sym); a Gaussian Fubini identity and conc_fun_sum conclude.
  • prod_gauss_le_cf_rV writes E[exp (- |z - Y|^2 / (2 s^2))] as a Gaussian average of cf_rV nu (cf_rV_regularization), after Ball and Nazarov. ball_le_cf_rV lets s tend to 0.
  • vector_small_ball_fourier combines Lemma 3.8 (cf_rV_regularized_le, from the Brascamp-Lieb inequality brascamp_lieb_ge0 with u_k = w_k / |w_k| and c_k = |w_k|^2), the removal of the Gaussian (ball_le_add_gauss) and a whitening matrix turning the w_k into a frame. vector_small_ball takes that matrix from whitening_exists and the constant from gauss_factor_exists.
  • cf_rV_inversion shows that Y + s G has the same law whether Y has law nu or cf_rV_inv_prob, and concludes with lipschitz_add_gauss and measure_lipschitz_unique.

Connections

  • Requires probability/lindeberg.v, seven files of gaussian/ (among them gaussian/gauss.v and gaussian/lebesgue_rV.v), matrix/euclidean.v, matrix/real_spectral.v, matrix/whitening.v, and five of comparison/, among them comparison/gauss_law.v and comparison/brascamp_lieb_lebesgue.v.
  • Used by matrix/covering.v and random_matrix/goe.v. Outside the library, extension/Sat/OneStage.v uses vector_small_ball_moments, and extension/Paired/ uses Rintegral_FTC and Rintegral_even of esseen.v. applications/ does not require it.

cf_rV_inversion
The Fourier inversion theorem on row vectors: if \int |cf_rV nu theta| dtheta < +oo against lebesgue_rV m, then nu X = \int_X cf_rV_density nu w dw for every measurable X, with cf_rV_density nu w = (2 pi)^-m \int (cos <theta, w> cf_rV_re nu theta + sin <theta, w> cf_rV_im nu theta) dtheta.

Gaussian regularization of the density: for s > 0, (2 pi)^-m \int exp (- s^2 |theta|^2 / 2) (...) dtheta is the density s^-m E[gauss_pdf ((w - Y) / s)] of Y + s G, it is the average of cf_rV_density nu over w + s G, and it tends to cf_rV_density nu w as s -> 0.
integral_add_gausslipschitz_add_gauss
For any law mu on 'rV[R]_m and G of law gauss m: E[phi (Y + s G)] = s^-m \int phi w E[gauss_pdf ((w - Y) / s)] dw for nonnegative measurable phi, and |E[phi (Y + s G)] - E[phi Y]| <= |L| s (1 + m) / 2 for phi with values in [0, 1] and Lipschitz with constant L.
