Spectral gaps on a finite space

For a finite type X and weights w x > 0, wdot w f g = sum_x w x f x g x is the inner product of L^2(w). The map f |-> (sqrt (w x) f x)_x (wvec, with inverse wfun) is an isometry of L^2(w) onto 'cV[R]_#|X| (mxdot_wvec), and an operator L on the functions on X has the matrix wop_mx w L in the basis wbasis that wvec maps to the canonical basis (wop_mx_mul, wop_mx_form).

Let L be linear, self-adjoint in L^2(w) and nonnegative: <f, L g>_w = <L f, g>_w and <f, L f>_w >= 0. If k <f, L f>_w <= <L f, L f>_w for every f, then

k <f, f>_w <= <f, L f>_w

for every f orthogonal to the kernel of L (wgap_from_square): the matrix of L is symmetric and positive semidefinite, and psd_square_gap of matrix/spectral_gap.v applies to it. If moreover the weights sum to one and the kernel of L is the constants, this is the Poincare inequality

k Var_w(f) <= <f, L f>_w

(wpoincare_from_square): a bound of the Dirichlet form by the norm of the generator gives a spectral gap.

Part of target T4 of phase 12: a Rocq formalization of the credited argument of the end of the proof of the proposition 'Gap and covariance under scaled interactions and fields' (rcut:prop:uniform-gap) of 'Cutoff throughout the high-temperature Sherrington-Kirkpatrick phase' (family 227 of the OpenAI math collection, revision 1581d4cbd, file appendices/entropy.tex). The manuscript reads ||A f||^2 >= kappa <f, A f> as a spectral gap of the self-adjoint nonnegative operator A, whose kernel is the constants, by applying it to the eigenfunctions; here the spectral theorem enters through the matrix of the operator. It follows the TeX, not a Lean module of the collection.

Main result, as the README states it

Spectral gaps on a finite space. For a finite type X, weights w x > 0 and an operator L on the functions on X that is linear, self-adjoint in L^2(w) (<f, L g>_w = <L f, g>_w, with <f, g>_w = sum_x w x f x g x) and nonnegative (<f, L f>_w >= 0): if k <f, L f>_w <= <L f, L f>_w for every f, then k <f, f>_w <= <f, L f>_w for every f orthogonal to the kernel of L. If moreover the weights sum to one and the kernel of L is the constants, then k Var_w(f) <= <f, L f>_w for every f (a Poincare inequality). A Rocq formalization of the credited argument of a proposition of family 227 of the OpenAI math collection (see the header).
