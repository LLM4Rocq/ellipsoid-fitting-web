GaussianAnalysis/comparison/

comparison/

GaussianAnalysis.comparison19 files12,644 lines439 lemmas and theorems, 47 definitions

Linear images and rotation invariance, shifts, tails, moment generating functions and chi-square laws, concentration of Lipschitz functions and the Poincare inequality, Slepian, Sudakov-Fernique and Gordon comparison inequalities, Sudakov's minoration, escape through a mesh, norms of Gaussian matrices, the convex Gaussian min-max theorem, the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and the Brascamp-Lieb inequality.

Guide

Written for this documentation from the sources and the README, and checked against the Rocq statements. The file headers and the statements are the reference.

This directory proves the classical inequalities for Gaussian vectors: linear images, rotations, shifts, tails, concentration of Lipschitz functions, the comparisons of Slepian, Sudakov-Fernique and Gordon, and Sudakov's minoration. It applies them to Gaussian matrices up to the convex Gaussian min-max theorem (CGMT), and ends with the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and the Brascamp-Lieb inequality. Random matrix, Fourier and subgaussian files of the library build on it.

The files rest on gauss n, the standard Gaussian measure on 'rV[R]_n (gaussian/), and on the normal laws normal_prob m s of MathComp-Analysis. Below, x and y have laws gauss n and gauss m, so x *m A is centered Gaussian with covariance A^T A; G = vec_mx a, a of law gauss (N * M), is an N x M matrix of independent standard normal entries; g and h are independent with laws gauss N and gauss M. |x| is the Euclidean norm frobenius x, <x, y> is mxdot, and F is L-Lipschitz when |F x - F y| <= L |x - y|.

Reading order

  1. normal_mgf.v: exponential moments and tails of normal_prob m s; Chernoff's bound.
  2. normal_linear.v: affine images of Lebesgue measure and normal laws; linear combinations of two independent normal variables.
  3. chi_square.v: exponential moments and tails of weighted centered chi-square sums.
  4. gauss_linear.v: law and tails of a linear form <x, a>.
  5. gauss_rotation.v: rotation invariance of two independent standard Gaussian vectors.
  6. gauss_shift.v: the Cameron-Martin formula.
  7. gauss_smoothing.v: Gaussian smoothing of bounded Lipschitz functions.
  8. gauss_concentration.v: concentration and the Poincare inequality for Lipschitz functions.
  9. slepian.v: the Sudakov-Fernique and Slepian inequalities.
  10. gordon.v: Gordon's min-max inequality.
  11. gauss_law.v: the law of x *m A depends only on A^T A.
  12. gauss_matrix_law.v: Gaussian matrices from independent Gaussian rows, and their transposes.
  13. escape.v: escape through a mesh and norms of Gaussian matrices.
  14. sudakov.v: Sudakov's minoration and the dual Sudakov inequality.
  15. cgmt.v: the CGMT in mean and concentration form.
  16. cone_escape.v: the CGMT on cones.
  17. ornstein_uhlenbeck.v: the Mehler transform and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup.
  18. brascamp_lieb.v: the Gaussian Brascamp-Lieb inequality for functions with values in [delta, K].
  19. brascamp_lieb_lebesgue.v: the extension to [0, +oo]-valued functions and the Lebesgue form.

Core definitions

  • gauss_smooth (gauss_smoothing.v): x |-> E[G (x + eps y)], y of law gauss n.
  • rmax and incr_var (slepian.v): the largest coordinate of a row vector; the variance of (x *m A)_j - (x *m A)_l, computed from A^T A.
  • minmax (gordon.v): the smallest of the row maxima of a matrix.
  • gwidth_fun and escape_inf (escape.v): sup_(x in T) <h, x>, whose mean is the Gaussian width of T, and inf_(x in T) |G x|.
  • cgmt_aux (cgmt.v): inf_(x in K) (|g| |x| - <h, x>)_+, the auxiliary value of the CGMT.
  • cone_sup (cone_escape.v): sup_(x in K, |x| <= 1) <h, x>.
  • mehler and ou (ornstein_uhlenbeck.v): mehler r f x = E[f (r x + sqrt (1 - r^2) Z)], Z standard normal; ou t f = mehler (exp (- t)) f.

Main results

The normal law.

  • integral_normal_expR, normal_prob_abs_tail: for X of law normal_prob m s, s != 0, E exp(t X) = exp(t m + t^2 s^2 / 2), and P(|X - m| >= u) <= 2 exp(- u^2 / (2 s^2)) for u >= 0.
  • normal_prob_lincomb: for independent X, Y of laws normal_prob m1 s1 and normal_prob m2 s2, with s1, s2, b nonzero, a X + b Y has law normal_prob (a m1 + b m2) (sqrt (a^2 s1^2 + b^2 s2^2)).

Gaussian vectors.

  • gauss_mxdot: for a != 0, <x, a> has law normal_prob 0 |a|.
  • integral_gauss_shift: E[f (x + h)] = E[f x exp(<h, x> - |h|^2 / 2)] for measurable f with extended real values.
  • gauss_mulmx_law, gauss_orthogonal_law: if A^T A = B^T B, x *m A and y *m B have the same law; if A^T A = 1, x *m A has law gauss k.

Concentration and comparison.

  • gauss_concentration, gauss_concentration_abs_tail, gauss_poincare_lipschitz: for F L-Lipschitz, E exp(t (F x - E F)) <= exp(t^2 L^2 / 2) for real t, P(|F x - E F| >= u) <= 2 exp(- u^2 / (2 L^2)) for u >= 0, and E[(F x - E F)^2] <= L^2.
  • sudakov_fernique, slepian: if incr_var A j l <= incr_var B j l for all j, l, then E rmax (x *m A) <= E rmax (y *m B); equal variances and (B^T B)_jl <= (A^T A)_jl suffice.
  • gordon: for X = vec_mx (x *m A) and Y = vec_mx (y *m B), if increments within a row have no larger variance in X than in Y and increments across distinct rows no smaller, E minmax X <= E minmax Y.

Gaussian matrices.

  • gordon_escape: for N > 0 and T a nonempty set of unit vectors of 'cV[R]_M, E inf_(x in T) |G x| >= E|g| - E gwidth_fun T h.
  • gauss_opnorm_mean_sqrt: E opnorm G <= sqrt N + sqrt M for N, M > 0.
  • cgmt_lower, cgmt_upper: for nonempty K in 'cV[R]_M with |x| <= rho on K, E escape_inf K G >= E cgmt_aux K g h - rho if N > 0, and E escape_inf K G <= E cgmt_aux K g h + rho if K is closed and convex.

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck and Brascamp-Lieb.

  • is_derive_ou_t, ou_cvg0_L1: for f measurable and bounded, d/dt ou t f = (ou t f)'' - x (ou t f)' for t > 0, and ou t f -> f in L^1 of the standard normal law as t -> 0+.
  • gauss_brascamp_lieb_bounded: for unit vectors u_i of 'rV[R]_m, c_i > 0 with sum_i c_i u_i^T u_i = 1, and measurable f_i with values in [delta, K], 0 < delta, int prod_i f_i(<x, u_i>)^(c_i) dgauss_m <= prod_i (int f_i dnormal_prob 0 1)^(c_i). gauss_brascamp_lieb allows measurable F_i : R -> [0, +oo], and brascamp_lieb is the Lebesgue form.

How the proofs go

  • Concentration follows Herbst. For F bounded and C^1 with gradient norm at most L, rotation against an independent copy and integration by parts give gauss_herbst_ineq, then a differential inequality for E exp(t F). Clipping, smoothing and Fatou's lemma reach every Lipschitz F.
  • sudakov_fernique and gordon apply Kahane's inequality gauss_comparison (gaussian/) to the smooth maximum lse b and the soft min-max smm b, and let b grow.
  • gauss_law_smooth applies gauss_comparison_partial in both directions to C^2 tests; double smoothing (gauss_law_lipschitz) and measure_lipschitz_unique then give gauss_mulmx_law.
  • escape.v applies gordon and sudakov_fernique to y^T G x and <g, y> + <h, x> on finite families of unit vectors, then passes to T with nets.
  • For Brascamp-Lieb, Lambda(r) = int exp (sum_i c_i ln (mehler r f_i)(<x, u_i>)) dgauss_m is the right side at r = 0 and the left side at r = 1. The generator identity, gauss_ibp_unit and tight_frame_cauchy_schwarz give r Lambda'(r) <= 0 on (0, 1).
