Let x have law gauss n, and let M t : 'M_(n, k) depend differentiably on a real parameter t. Then x *m M t is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance (M t)^T *m M t. For F : 'rV_k -> R of class C^2 whose derivatives of order at most 2 grow at most polynomially, gauss_interpolation gives the derivative of t |-> E[F (x *m M t)]:

\sum_(j, l) ((M' t)^T *m M t) j l * E[d_l d_j F (x *m M t)],

with M' the derivative of M and d_j F = 'D_(delta_mx 0 j) F the partial derivative along the j-th vector of the canonical basis. When the Hessian of F is symmetric, this is half the pairing of the derivative of the covariance with the expected Hessian.

gauss_comparison is Kahane's comparison inequality: if

\sum_(j, l) (A^T *m A - B^T *m B) j l * d_l d_j F z <= 0

for every z, then E[F (x *m A)] <= E[F (y *m B)] for x of law gauss n and y of law gauss m. The inequalities of Slepian, Sudakov-Fernique and Gordon follow from it for suitable F. The proof applies gauss_interpolation to the path col_mx (cos t *: A) (sin t *: B), t in [0, pi / 2], whose ends give x *m A and y *m B for the two blocks of a vector of law gauss (n + m).

gauss_comparison_partial is the same inequality for F given by its partial derivatives of order 1 and 2 at every point, those of order 2 continuous: F is then of class C^2 (differentiable_partials of partial_derivative.v).

The first sections hold general facts used by the proofs: the chain rule along a curve, the derivative of a matrix product along a path, and integrals of finite sums.

Gaussian interpolation: let x have law gauss n, let the path of matrices M t be differentiable on an open interval with derivative M' t, with |M t| <= K and |M' t| <= K on the interval, and let F be of class C^2 with F and its partial derivatives of order 1 and 2 all bounded by one C (1 + |z|^p). Kahane's comparison inequality, for F of class C^2 with the same growth hypothesis: if \sum_(j, l) (A^T A - B^T B)_(j, l) d_l d_j F <= 0 everywhere, then E[F (x *m A)] <= E[F (y *m B)] for x of law gauss n and y of law gauss m.

The same comparison for F given by its partial derivatives of order 1 and 2, those of order 2 continuous.
