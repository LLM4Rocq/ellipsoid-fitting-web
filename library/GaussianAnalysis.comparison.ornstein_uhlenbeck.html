For f : R -> R bounded and measurable, the Mehler transform mehler r f x is E[f (r x + sqrt (1 - r^2) Z)] for Z of law normal_prob 0 1, and the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup is ou t f = mehler (expR (- t)) f (Mehler's formula, ouE). Then mehler 1 f = f and mehler 0 f is the mean of f (mehler1, mehler0, ou0), mehler r f keeps the bounds of f (mehler_le, mehler_ge), the standard normal law is invariant (integral_mehler) and mehler r is a contraction of L^1 of that law (mehler_contraction).

For |r| < 1 the law of r x + sqrt (1 - r^2) Z has the density mehler_kernel r x against normal_prob 0 1 (mehler_kernel_integral): the exponential of a quadratic polynomial in x and y. Differentiating it under the integral (is_derive_normal_kernel) gives the derivatives of mehler r f of order 1, 2 and 3 in x and the derivative in r (is_derive_mehler, is_derive_mehler_d1, is_derive_mehler_d2, is_derive_mehler_r), written as integrals against Hermite polynomials. They satisfy the generator identity r d/dr mehler r f = x (mehler r f)' - (mehler r f)'' (mehler_generator); for t > 0 it reads d/dt ou t f = (ou t f)'' - x (ou t f)' (is_derive_ou_t). The derivatives are bounded (mehler_d1_le, mehler_d2_le, mehler_dr_le) and continuous (continuous_mehler, continuous_mehler_d1, continuous_mehler_d2). The kernel gives the derivative in t as well as those in x, so it replaces the Cameron-Martin formula, which only shifts x.

As t -> +oo, ou t f x tends to the mean of f (ou_cvgy). As t -> 0 from the right, ou t f x tends to f x when f is continuous (ou_cvg0, mehler_cvg1), and ou t f tends to f in L^1 of the standard normal law for every bounded measurable f (ou_cvg0_L1, mehler_cvg_L1). The last limit goes through the density of bounded continuous functions in that space (normal_continuous_approx), which follows from the density of continuous functions in L^1 of Lebesgue measure on a segment (approximation_continuous_integrable of MathComp-Analysis).
