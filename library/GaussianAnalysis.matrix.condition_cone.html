For C >= 1, cond_cone C is the set of the real symmetric n x n matrices Q with t I <= Q <= C t I for some t >= 0: the positive semidefinite matrices of condition number at most C, and 0. It is a closed convex cone. Its distance to a symmetric matrix H and its support function are computed from the spectrum of H: the nearest point of the cone clips the eigenvalues of H to an interval [t, C t] (Tanaka and Nakata, Optim. Lett. 8 (2014)). The optimal t is not needed in closed form: the distance is an infimum over t >= 0 of a sum over the eigenvalues, which is what a limit theorem for random spectra can handle.

  • itv_dist a b x, clamp a b x: the distance from x to [a, b] and the nearest point; itv_dist_clamp, itv_dist_le (the distance to any point of [a, b] is larger), itv_dist_lipschitz (1-Lipschitz in x), itv_dist_shift (Lipschitz in the endpoints).
  • cond_coneP: for Q symmetric, Q is in the cone iff its eigenvalues lie in some [t, C t] with t >= 0; cond_cone_quad: for C >= 0, iff Q is symmetric, positive semidefinite and |y|^2 x^T Q x <= C |x|^2 y^T Q y for all x, y; cond_cone0, cond_cone1, cond_coneZ, cond_cone_convex.
  • cond_cone_vec C, the cone in the coordinates of mxvec: cond_cone_vec_closed, cond_cone_vec0, cond_cone_vec_convex, cond_cone_vecZ, the hypotheses of the cone projection of convex/moreau.v.
  • cond_cone_dist: for H symmetric and C >= 1, the squared Frobenius distance from H to the cone is cond_dist C H, the infimum over t >= 0 of cond_energy C t H = sum_i dist(lambda_i(H), [t, C t])^2. cond_energy_le (Hoffman-Wielandt) gives the lower bound, cond_clip and cond_clip_energy (the eigenvalues of H clipped to [t, C t]) the upper bound.
  • cond_support C A, the supremum of <A, Q> over the Q of the cone with |Q|_F <= 1: cond_support_proj, it is the norm of the projection of A onto the cone; cond_support_sqr, for H symmetric and C >= 1, cond_support C H ^ 2 = |H|_F^2 - cond_dist C H (Moreau's decomposition); cond_support_sym, only the symmetric part of A matters; cond_supportZ, cond_energyZ, cond_distZ, the scalings.
  • cond_energy_mono, cond_dist_itv: when the eigenvalues of H are at most T >= 0, the energy increases on [T, oo) and the infimum may be taken over [0, T].
  • cond_slice C, the trace slice {Q in the cone | tr Q = n}, a compact convex base of the cone: cond_slice1, cond_slice_spec (eigenvalues in [1/C, C]), cond_slice_t (between t I and C t I with t in [1/C, 1]), cond_slice_form, cond_slice_frobenius (n <= |Q|_F^2 <= C n), cond_cone_slice (every nonzero point of the cone has a positive multiple in the slice); cond_slice_vec, the slice in the coordinates of mxvec: cond_slice_vec_closed, cond_slice_vec_convex, cond_slice_vec_frobenius, cond_cone_vec_slice.

The squared distance from H to the cone.

Main result, as the README states it

For C >= 1 and H symmetric, the squared Frobenius distance from H to cond_cone C is cond_dist C H, the infimum over t >= 0 of sum_i dist(lambda_i(H), [t, C t])^2: the lower bound by Hoffman-Wielandt, the upper bound by clipping the eigenvalues of H to [t, C t] (Tanaka and Nakata). The support function, the supremum of <H, Q> over the Q of the cone with |Q|_F <= 1, is the norm of the projection of H onto the cone, and its square is |H|_F^2 - cond_dist C H (Moreau's decomposition). Only the symmetric part of a matrix matters, the support function and cond_dist scale, and when the eigenvalues of H are at most T >= 0 the infimum over t may be taken over [0, T].

A matrix of the slice is between t I and C t I for some t in [1/C, 1].

Main result, as the README states it

The trace slice {Q in cond_cone C : tr Q = n} is a closed convex base of the cone: for C > 0 and n >= 1 its matrices satisfy t I <= Q <= C t I for some t in [1/C, 1], have eigenvalues in [1/C, C] and n <= |Q|_F^2 <= C n, and every nonzero point of the cone has a positive multiple in it.
